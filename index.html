About the NLP4Healthcare

Continuing the workshop series formerly known as NLP-AI4Health (2024 and 2025), NLP4Healthcare 2027 marks the third edition of the workshop under its updated title. It is grounded in the recognition that Healthcare communication is fundamentally a language problem as well as a clinical one. In multilingual and resource-constrained settings, patients may describe symptoms using regional languages, colloquial expressions, non-standard spellings, and code-mixed speech, while clinicians must transform these interactions into accurate and actionable clinical records. Yet much of clinical language technology is developed and evaluated on relatively clean, predominantly monolingual text, leaving a substantial gap between benchmark conditions and real-world communication.

Jointly organized by the Language Technology Research Centre (LTRC), IIIT Hyderabad and Christian Medical College (CMC) Vellore, NLP4Healthcare 2027 addresses this gap by bringing together researchers in natural language processing, computational linguistics, speech and language technologies, artificial intelligence, and healthcare. The workshop focuses on multilingual, patient-centred, culturally aware, and clinically useful language technologies, with particular emphasis on Indian languages and real-world clinical communication.

The 2027 edition places a special focus on code-mixed Indian-language doctor–patient dialogues. Clinical encounters in India may involve rapid switching between regional languages and English medical terminology, together with conversational phenomena that are difficult to represent using conventional NLP pipelines. Such data raise research questions in clinical information extraction, dialogue understanding, documentation, summarization, speech processing, translation, evaluation, and responsible deployment.

Scope

We invite original research papers, short papers, system descriptions, and non-archival position papers addressing the intersection of NLP, AI, speech, and healthcare communication. Topics include, but are not limited to:

  • Clinical information extraction, medical entity recognition, term identification, normalization, and relation extraction from clinical conversations.
  • Clinical dialogue understanding, including conversational structure, symptom and medication understanding, and clinically relevant dialogue phenomena.
  • Automated clinical documentation, including dialogue-to-SOAP generation, clinical record synthesis, and discharge-summary generation.
  • Medical dialogue summarization with emphasis on factuality, completeness, and preservation of clinically important information.
  • Multilingual and low-resource medical machine translation across Indian and other underrepresented languages.
  • Medical question answering, patient-facing dialogue systems, and multilingual health-information access.
  • Speech technologies for healthcare, including ASR, TTS, spoken-language understanding, and voice-enabled clinical documentation.
  • Health misinformation detection, verification, and mitigation in regional languages.
  • Ethics, privacy, safety, fairness, bias, de-identification, and cultural sensitivity in healthcare NLP and AI.
  • Datasets, benchmarks, annotation methodologies, evaluation frameworks, and real-world deployment studies.

Objectives

  • Characterize linguistic and clinical challenges in multilingual and code-mixed healthcare communication.
  • Advance methods for extracting, structuring, translating, summarizing, and generating clinically useful information from spoken and written interactions.
  • Establish benchmarks that evaluate models under realistic low-resource and code-mixed conditions.
  • Connect communities of NLP researchers, clinicians, speech researchers, healthcare practitioners, and related stakeholders.
  • Promote responsible research that explicitly addresses privacy, safety, fairness, and cultural appropriateness.

Shared Tasks: Code-Mixed Indian Language Doctor–Patient Dialogue Processing & Simplified Translation of Medical Texts

Shared Task 1: Code-Mixed Indian Language Doctor–Patient Dialogue Processing

The shared task is designed as a central empirical component of the workshop. It introduces a benchmark of code-mixed doctor–patient dialogues across six Indian languages: Hindi, Bengali, Kannada, Marathi, Tamil, and Telugu. The tasks are deliberately complementary: they test whether systems can first identify clinically meaningful language, then structure the interaction into a clinical representation, and finally produce a concise clinically useful summary.

Sub-Task 1.1: Medical Term Identification (MTI) in Code-Mixed Conversations

Given code-mixed doctor-patient dialogue transcripts, participants must accurately identify, extract, and categorize medical terms (symptoms, diagnoses, medications, anatomical sites, procedures).

Sub-Task 1.2: Automated SOAP Note Generation from Clinical Dialogues

Participants automatically structure unstructured doctor-patient interactions into standardized clinical SOAP notes:

  • S (Subjective): Patient's reported chief complaints and history.
  • O (Objective): Vital signs, physical exam findings, test results.
  • A (Assessment): Preliminary or differential diagnosis.
  • P (Plan): Prescribed treatment, medication, tests, follow-up.

Sub-Task 1.3: Medical Dialogue Summarization

Generate concise, coherent, and clinically accurate summaries suitable for physician quick-review or discharge documentation.

Shared Task 2: Simplified Translation of Medical Texts

Text Simplification helps bridge communication gaps, especially in the context of a specialized domain like Medicine where medical texts are often too dense and difficult to interpret for laypeople. Furthermore, in a multilingual context such as that of Indic languages, translation and simplification are two communication tools that are often deployed together. With this context in mind, we propose a shared task that involves simplification and translation of medical text. This shared task evaluates models on medical text simplification and simplification-translation tasks. Participants may compete in either or both sub-tasks:

Sub-Task 2.1: Monolingual Medical Text Simplification

Given a complex medical text, systems must generate a simplified version in the same language (Lcomplex → Lsimplified), e.g., English (Complex) → English (Simplified) or Hindi (Complex) → Hindi (Simplified).

Key Challenge: Reducing syntactic complexity and replacing dense medical jargon with accessible terminology without altering underlying clinical meaning.

Sub-Task 2.2: Cross-Lingual Simplified Medical Translation

Given a complex medical text in a source language, systems must simultaneously translate and simplify the content into a target language (L1complex → L2simplified), e.g., English (Complex) → Hindi (Simplified).

Key Challenge: Performing dual-task transformation (cross-lingual translation + style/readability adaptation) while maintaining strict factual and clinical fidelity.

Data & Evaluation

Annotated training and development datasets will be provided for all sub-tasks. System outputs will be evaluated using automated text simplification and machine translation metrics, complemented by expert human evaluations on a sampled subset to assess clinical accuracy, readability, and translation quality.

Detailed task guidelines, evaluation metrics, baseline systems, dataset release timelines, and participant estimates will be released soon.
